# Advisor outreach: one construction or legal person for the team

Why: the scorecard gives the team 1 out of 4 because nobody has construction or legal experience. One credible advisor is the fastest way to move that to 2 and to make the pilot interviews possible. This is a draft for you to send; nothing has been sent.

## Who to look for (any one is enough)

| Profile | What they add | Where to find them in Shymkent |
|---|---|---|
| Owner or project manager of a mid-size general contractor | Real contract clauses, retention terms, introductions to subcontractors | Local construction associations, university alumni, family contacts, the bootcamp mentors |
| Construction lawyer or in-house counsel | Answers the questions in [LEGAL.md](LEGAL.md) | Law faculty at your university, regional bar association |
| Technical supervisor (the "arbiter" role) | Defect and acceptance practice | Engineering inspection firms, university civil-engineering faculty |
| Bank or surety officer who writes retention bonds | Price benchmark for the alternative | Local banks' construction lending desks |

## What you ask for (small and concrete)

- 30 minutes to read one page and tell us what is wrong with it.
- Show one real retention clause (names removed).
- Introduce us to two subcontractors.
- If they like it: be named as an advisor on the team slide (no money, no equity promised).

## Message to a contractor (English; translate to Russian or Kazakh before sending)

> Hello [Name],
>
> I am a student at Tashenev University building a small open-source tool for a problem every subcontractor knows: the retention (5–10% held back for a year or two) that comes back late or not at all, especially when the client goes bankrupt. The tool keeps the retention in a vault that nobody, including the client, can touch until the warranty ends, and then releases it automatically.
>
> I am not selling anything. I have no construction experience, and I would be grateful for 30 minutes of your time to tell me where this is wrong. If it makes sense to you, I would like to ask for one thing: to see how a real retention clause is written in your contracts.
>
> Here is a 90-second demo video: [link]. Thank you.
>
> [Name, phone, university]

## Message to a lawyer

> Hello [Name],
>
> I am building an open-source prototype that holds construction retention money in a Solana program vault. I wrote down five legal questions I cannot answer (trust status of the vault in the client's insolvency, assignment of the claim, licensing). Could you spare 30 minutes to tell me which of them are the real issues? The list is one page: [link to LEGAL.md]. This is a student project; I am asking for guidance, not paid work.
>
> [Name, phone, university]

## After the call (same day)

1. Write down what they said in their words, with the date.
2. Ask: "Who else should I speak to?" and "Can I name you as an advisor?"
3. Add them to the team slide only after they say yes in writing (a message is enough).

## Do not

- Do not promise equity or money.
- Do not claim they endorse the product; say "advisor" only if they agreed.
- Do not send real contract names or numbers into the public repo.
