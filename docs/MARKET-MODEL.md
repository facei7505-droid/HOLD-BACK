# Market model (bottom-up, with the assumptions marked)

Fact = sourced. Assumption = our guess, to be replaced by interview answers. The same formulas run in the calculator on the website.

## Formula

```
retention flow per year   = construction output × subcontracted share × share of subcontracts with retention × retention rate
retention held on average = retention flow × average months held / 12
yearly fee pool           = retention held on average × fee rate
```

## Kazakhstan (first test market)

| Input | Value | Type |
|---|---|---|
| Construction output, 2025 | 10.705 trillion ₸ (+15.9% on 2024) | Fact: [Bureau of National Statistics via news summary](https://stat.gov.kz/en/industries/business-statistics/stat-inno-build/publications/506289/) |
| Private firms' share of that output | 87.9% | Fact: same source |
| Subcontracted share of output | 40% | Assumption A1 |
| Subcontracts that carry a retention clause | 50% | Assumption A2 |
| Retention rate | 5% | Assumption A3 (common in contracts; to confirm in interviews) |
| Average months held | 12 | Assumption A4 (warranty is often 12–24 months) |
| Fee on held amount per year | 0.5% | Assumption A5 (our pricing hypothesis) |

Result with the assumptions above:

| Output | Value |
|---|---|
| Retention flow per year | 10.705 trillion × 0.40 × 0.50 × 0.05 = **about 107 billion ₸** |
| Held on average | **about 107 billion ₸** (about $0.2 bn at roughly 500 ₸ per $; check today's rate) |
| Yearly fee pool at 0.5% | **about 0.5 billion ₸**, about $1 m |

**What this says, honestly:** Kazakhstan alone is a small revenue pool for a fee on the held amount (about $1 m a year if every contract used Holdback). It is a good place to run the first pilot, not the market. The pitch must stay global.

## United States (formula only, no sourced total yet)

Typical retainage is 5–10% of each progress payment; averages reported in academic work are 7.59% on private, 5.56% on state and 3.26% on federal projects ([summary by Corpay](https://www.corpay.com/resources/blog/construction-retainage)). To size the US pool, multiply annual construction spending by the subcontracted share, the retainage rate and months held / 12. Needs a sourced spending figure (US Census) before it goes into a pitch.

## A second revenue line: early cash for subcontractors

The claim sale creates a financing market. If 10% of the held balance in Kazakhstan were sold early at a 10% discount (the funder earns about 11% over the holding period), the discount pool is about 0.1 × 107 bn × 10% = **about 1 billion ₸ a year**, twice the pool from the 0.5% fee. A platform fee of 1–2% of the sale price on top is possible. Both numbers are assumptions.

## What to replace with real answers

| Assumption | Interview question |
|---|---|
| A1 subcontracted share | "What share of your project value do you hand to subcontractors?" |
| A2 retention clause | "Do your subcontract agreements hold back a percentage? Can I see the clause?" |
| A3 rate | "How much is held back, as a percentage?" |
| A4 months held | "How long after finishing does the money come back? Last three projects?" |
| A5 fee | "What would you pay to make sure the money comes back on the due date?" |
