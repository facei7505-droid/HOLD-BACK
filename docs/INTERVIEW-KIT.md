# Interview kit: 10 conversations in 5 days

Goal: find out whether retention is a real, costly problem for the person in front of you, and get one concrete commitment from each. Do not pitch the product first.

**Who:** 7 subcontractors (electrical, plumbing, finishing, windows, roofing; 5–30 people) and 3 developers or general contractors. Source them from 2GIS, OLX, Instagram, building sites near your university, family contacts. Ask each person for one more introduction.

**Format:** 15 minutes, in person or by phone. Take notes with exact quotes and numbers.

## Opening (30 seconds)

"I am a student working on a project about how construction payments work. I am not selling anything. I would like to learn how you handle the part of the payment that the client holds back until the warranty ends."

## Subcontractor script

1. Tell me about your last project. How was it paid? Does the client hold back a percentage?
2. How much, as a percentage? Can I see the clause in the contract? (Ask for a photo with prices blacked out.)
3. How long after you finished did the money come back? And on the project before that?
4. Has it ever been late, partly paid, or not paid? What did you do? What did it cost you?
5. How much of your money is held back right now, in total?
6. What do you do when you need cash and it is stuck? (loans, advances, delays to your own suppliers)
7. If that money sat in a vault the client could not touch, and was released automatically on the due date, what would change for you?
8. Would you sell your claim for 90% of its value today? When yes, when no?
9. Would you sign a short letter saying you would try this on one contract? (Show the template below.)
10. Who else should I talk to?

## Developer / general contractor script

1. How do you pay subcontractors? Do you hold back a percentage, how much, for how long?
2. What happens to the held money: separate account, same account, bank guarantee instead?
3. How often do subcontractors ask for it early or complain about delays?
4. What would make you agree to put it into a vault you cannot touch? (a client requirement, a law, better subcontractors, a rating, a fee discount)
5. What would you pay per year, as a percentage of the held amount, for the vault to run by itself with a report for your auditor?
6. Would you run a pilot on one contract with test money first?

## What counts as evidence of demand

| Signal | Weight |
|---|---|
| Showed the clause or a payment record | strong |
| Said the exact amount stuck and the months | strong |
| Signed the letter of intent | strongest |
| Agreed to a pilot call with the other party | strong |
| "Interesting idea" | nothing |

**Go / no-go after 10 conversations:** go if at least 5 of 7 subcontractors confirm money held back and at least one delay of more than 3 months, and at least 1 developer is open to a pilot. Otherwise change the target country (New Zealand, Australia) or the idea.

## Letter of intent (non-binding)

> We, [company], confirm that we would like to test Holdback on one construction contract of about [amount] with test funds, starting [month 2026]. This letter does not bind either side. Signed: [name, role, date].

## Outreach messages

**English**

> Hi [name], I am a student researching how retention money works in construction in Kazakhstan. Could I ask you 15 minutes of questions about your last project? I am not selling anything. If it helps, I will share what I learn from other contractors.

**Русский**

> Здравствуйте, [имя]! Я студент, изучаю, как в строительстве работает гарантийное удержание по договорам подряда. Можно задать 15 минут вопросов про ваш последний проект? Ничего не продаю, поделюсь тем, что узнаю у других подрядчиков.

## Tracking sheet (columns)

date, name, role, company size, city, retention % held, months held, amount stuck now, a delay or loss story (quote), asked for clause (yes/no), letter signed (yes/no), pilot interest (yes/no), introduction to, notes.
