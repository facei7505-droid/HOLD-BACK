How it works today

The client keeps 5% of every payment "as a warranty" for 1–2 years

$190,000 paid to the subcontractor
$10,000 held back

A $200,000 contract. The held-back $10,000 sits in the client's own account, mixed with their other money.

Delayed

The warranty ends and the answer is "next month". The subcontractor chases the money.

Spent

For the client it is free working capital for the next project.

Lost

If the client goes bankrupt, the retention goes to their creditors. As happened with Carillion in 2018.
Why now

Governments admit the problem, but their fixes are blunt

New Zealand · 2023
Law: retention must be held in trust in a separate bank account, with a ledger per contract.
Costly, and still controlled by the party that owes the money.
United Kingdom · 2025
After a consultation, cash retentions are to be banned, not before 2027.
The client loses the quality guarantee.
Kazakhstan · 2025–26
KZTE tenge stablecoin pilot and a KASE × Solana Foundation MoU on tokenization.
Money is arriving on-chain in the local currency.

Holdback is the third way: the retention stays, but it cannot be abused.

The solution

Four rules enforced by a program, not by people

01 · SPLIT
Each invoice is split in one transaction: 95% to the subcontractor, 5% into the vault.
→
02 · VAULT
The vault is a program address with no key. The client cannot take it back.
→
03 · DEFECT
Only the cost of a defect is frozen. An arbiter settles disputes.
→
04 · RELEASE
After the date, anyone clicks a Blink and the money goes to its owner.

No calls, no begging, no lawsuits. The subcontractor sees the money in real time.

What a bank cannot do

Retention becomes an asset you can sell today

The subcontractor needs cash now, not in 2 years. They list their claim on the vault at 90%.

A funder pays, and the claim moves to them in the same transaction. Nobody can take the money without handing over the claim.

When the warranty ends, the vault pays the funder.

Subcontractor gets now$4,050
Funder receives at the end$4,500
Funder's return+11%
Funder's riskthe money is already in the vault, not a promise to pay
How we use Solana

A real program, not a wallet button

Anchor program

8 instructions: create and accept contract, pay with split, raise defect, resolve, list claim, buy claim, release.

PDA vault

The vault has no private key. Only the program can sign a transfer, and only by the rules.

Atomicity

Splitting a payment and selling a claim are single transactions: all or nothing.

Token-2022

Works with SPL Token and plain Token-2022 mints, such as USDC. Risky token extensions are refused.

Solana Actions

The release button is a Blink: a link anyone can click.

Cost

0.000005 SOL per action. The whole 8-transaction scenario costs a fraction of a cent.

What we checked

Security and law: fixed and still open

Security self-review

4 weaknesses found and fixed with tests: arbiter chosen by the client alone, a silent arbiter locking the vault, buyer exposed to a shrinking vault, token extensions that break payouts.
Open: not audited; the upgrade key must go to a multisig before real money; a stablecoin issuer can freeze a vault.

Legal

Is the vault safe if the client goes bankrupt? Courts have protected retention held on trust in a separate fund. Nobody has ruled on a program-owned vault.
Open: a contract clause that declares the trust, a lawyer's opinion, rules on claim sales and stablecoins. Devnet and test tokens until then.
Prototype, not audited. Details: docs/THREAT-MODEL.md, docs/LEGAL.md
